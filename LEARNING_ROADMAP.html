Learning Roadmap

Physics of Fluid Neural Networks — Cranfield IRP 2025-26

1 Fundamentals of Acoustics

What You Need to Know

  • Wave equation (first-order and second-order forms)
  • Sound speed in gases: c = sqrt(gamma * R * T)
  • Acoustic impedance: Z = rho * c
  • Reflection and transmission at interfaces

Resources

  • Textbook: Kinsler & Frey, "Fundamentals of Acoustics" — Chapters 1-4
  • Textbook: Beranek & Mellow, "Acoustics" — Chapter 3 (reflection/transmission)
  • Online: sengpielaudio.com — Temperature vs Sound Speed
Key Question: Derive c = c0 * sqrt(T/T0) for air from the ideal gas law. Show your steps.

2 FDTD Numerical Methods

What You Need to Know

  • Yee staggered grid layout
  • Leapfrog time stepping
  • CFL stability condition: dt < dx / (c * sqrt(2))
  • Numerical dispersion and grid resolution
  • Absorbing boundary conditions

Resources

  • Textbook: Taflove & Hagness, "Computational Electrodynamics" — Chapter 1 (1D FDTD)
  • Lecture Notes: Purdue ECE 604 — FDTD and Yee Algorithm (PDF)
  • Paper: arXiv 2025 — "Acoustic Wave Modeling Using 2D FDTD" (modern code examples)
Key Question: Why does the staggered grid eliminate the need for interpolation when computing spatial derivatives?

3 Bragg Gratings & Periodic Structures

What You Need to Know

  • Bragg condition: d = m * lambda / 2
  • Band gaps in periodic media
  • Coupling coefficient kappa
  • Reflection from finite gratings: R = tanh^2(kappa * L)

Resources

  • Tutorial: "Bragg waveguide grating as a 1D photonic band gap" (PDF)
  • Tutorial: Ansys — Bragg Grating Design with FDTD
  • Textbook: Joannopoulos et al., "Photonic Crystals" — Chapters 4-5
Key Question: For our 20-period grating with r=0.17, verify that R = tanh^2(20 * 0.17) ≈ 0.997. Why did the simulation give R ≈ 1?

4 Physical Neural Networks (THE CORE)

What You Need to Know

  • Wave propagation as neural network forward pass
  • Material properties = network weights
  • Training physical systems via optimization
  • Types: diffractive networks, reservoir computing, mechanical networks

Read In This Order

  • 1. Hughes et al. 2019 (hughes2019wave) — "Wave physics as an analog recurrent neural network" (Sci. Adv.)
    START HERE. This is the foundation of your entire thesis. Read Sections 1-3 carefully.
  • 2. Wright et al. 2022 (wright2022deep) — "Deep physical neural networks trained with backpropagation" (Nature)
    How they physically built and trained a network. Focus on Methods.
  • 3. Stern et al. 2021 (stern2021physical) — "Supervised learning in physical networks" (Phys. Rev. X)
    Theoretical framework. Read the introduction and conclusions.
  • 4. Zangeneh-Nejad et al. 2021 (zangenehnejad2021analogue) — "Analogue computing with metamaterials" (Nat. Rev. Mater.)
    Broad review. Good for situating your work in the field.
Key Question: In Hughes' paper, what is the mathematical mapping between Maxwell's equations and the RNN state update? Write the correspondence for your acoustic variables (p, u, v).

5 Porous Media Acoustics

What You Need to Know

  • Biot theory: fast and slow waves
  • Equivalent fluid models (Zwikker-Kosten, JCA)
  • Darcy drag: -(mu/K) * u
  • Frequency-dependent attenuation

Resources

  • Textbook: Allard & Atalla, "Propagation of Sound in Porous Media" — Chapters 1-3
  • Paper: Zhao et al. 2018 — "Equivalent fluid model FDTD for porous material" (JASA)
  • Paper: Moufid et al. 2022 — "Time-domain EFM for rigid porous media"
Key Question: How does Darcy drag create frequency-dependent attenuation? (Hint: the drag force is proportional to velocity, but the pressure-velocity relationship depends on frequency.)

6 Thermoacoustics

What You Need to Know

  • Sound speed vs temperature
  • Thermal diffusion timescale
  • Steady-state heat equation
  • Quasi-static approximation

Resources

  • Textbook: Swift, "Thermoacoustics" — Chapter 2
  • Online: sengpielaudio.com — Temperature calculator
Key Question: Show that tau_thermal / tau_acoustic ~ 10^6 for air in your domain. This justifies the quasi-static assumption.

7 Optimization in Physical Systems

What You Need to Know

  • Gradient-free methods: random search, Nelder-Mead
  • Gradient-based: backpropagation through physics (adjoint method)
  • Why L-BFGS-B for bounded, medium-dimensional problems
  • Challenges: non-convexity, local minima

Resources

  • Review: Momeni et al. 2024 — "Training of Physical Neural Networks" (PDF)
Key Question: Why can't we easily use gradient descent for FDTD? What would backpropagation through time require?
