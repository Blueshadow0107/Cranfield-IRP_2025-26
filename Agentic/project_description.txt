Project 11: Fluid Neural Networks – Computing over Continuous Reactions
Company: Research
Supervisors:
Prof. Weisi Guo
Prof. Takis Tsoutsanis

Project Details (~150 words): traditional silicon-based neural networks (NN) have discrete
processing units (neurons with activation functions) and connecting weights. The architecture
is usually fixed and layer-by-layer (liquid NN have flexible architecture). The parameters of
weights are trained over sample data. The problem with this approach is that for each NN, it
can only make batched inference in sequence. This means a high number of users will have a
queue issue and this has raised the demand for global investment in GPU/CPU at edge. This
research proposes to examine the possibility of a completely different NN formulation, one
that is based in fluid dynamics and fluid reactions:

A. Continuous rather than discrete, where the mathematical processing takes place across
the whole architecture space
B. Parallelism rather than sequential processing, where parallel non-reactive chemicals
can allow multiple inference processes

Point A) has the potential of bringing new ML capability that is beyond current discrete NNs,
and Point B) can reduce the time of batch inference.

This involves 3 phases:
1. Modeling a controllable physical space (with input fluids and output detection of
mixture) that can control how reactive fluids mix.
2. Train the control units of physical space to learn to encode the network for parallel
tasks.
3. Test this performance on real settings against silicon networks.

The student should be highly motivated with a very strong CFD background and a passion for
basic machine learning.
