## 1. Architectural pivot & literature synthesis

### 1.1 Core architectural pivot

- Move away from static neuromorphic substrates (solid‑state electronic/ionic relaxation) and chaotic chemical soups.  
- Focus instead on **continuous analog wave computation** in fluids, using pressure (acoustic) waves as the information carrier.  
- Computation happens as the wave propagates; this sidesteps the von Neumann bottleneck by processing data "in flight" rather than in discrete clocked steps.

### 1.2 Three pillars of the architecture

1. **The Medium – Continuous propagation**  
   - Fluid domain supporting acoustic waves.  
   - Information encoded in amplitude, phase, frequency and spatial mode structure of pressure waves.

2. **The Math – Physics as an analog RNN**  
   - Governing PDEs (linear / weakly nonlinear acoustic wave equations) act as a **continuous‑depth recurrent neural network** (Neural ODE / analog RNN).  
   - Superposition and interference implement linear transformations; boundary conditions and geometry embed recurrence and memory.

3. **The Architecture – Spatial parallelism via structure**  
   - Instead of wiring 2D microfluidic channels, use **structured media** where tortuous pathways and obstacles act as diffractive elements.  
   - In 3D, a porous matrix plays the role of a **diffractive neural network**: the geometry implements effective weights by delaying and scattering waves.

### 1.3 Key precedent papers (anchor references)

- **Microfluidic bubble logic (Prakash & Gershenfeld)** – hydrodynamic interactions and bubbles can implement universal Boolean logic.  
- **Wave physics as analog RNN (Hughes et al., 2019)** – inhomogeneous wave media trained via backpropagation to recognize patterns.  
- **Diffractive deep neural networks (Lin et al., 2018)** – physical diffractive layers performing spatial matrix multiplication for classification tasks.  
- These precedents justify the claim that structured wave media can be used as trained computational substrates.


## 2. Core research questions (master's scope)

This section articulates explicit questions/hypotheses to be answered by August 2026.

1. **Y‑junction logic unit test**  
   *RQ1:* Can a 2D acoustic Y‑junction implement simple logic operations (e.g. AND / XOR) via phase control of inputs, with quantitatively measurable contrast between logical states (e.g. output amplitude ratio above/below thresholds)?

2. **Diffractive slice as a small analog network**  
   *RQ2:* Can a 2D array of obstacles ("diffractive slice") be configured to route wave energy to specified output locations, implementing a non‑trivial linear mapping between input and output ports?

3. **Geometry/medium optimization (minimal "training")**  
   *RQ3:* Within a small design space (e.g. obstacle positions/radii or porosity parameters), can simple numerical optimization methods improve a task‑level objective (e.g. maximize energy at target output, minimize at others)?

4. **3D porous medium as scalable architecture (stretch goal)**  
   *RQ4:* Can the validated 2D diffractive logic be extruded or generalized to a 3D porous medium in a way that preserves the computational behaviour and suggests a scalable fluid analog RNN architecture?

5. **Physical concept (optional experimental component)**  
   *RQ5:* Can the simulated architectures be translated into a realistic physical concept (dimensions, frequencies, materials, actuation/sensing) that could be implemented in a laboratory setting, even if full fabrication is beyond the master’s scope?


## 3. Simulation strategy – Y‑junction unit test

Goal: Establish a **numerically trustworthy, physically interpretable unit cell** for wave‑based logic, and use it to calibrate the CFD setup.

### 3.1 Geometry and operating regime

- 2D Y‑junction geometry with two inlets and one outlet.  
- Characteristic length scale: order of 0.5 mm in initial concept (can be rescaled for numerical convenience).  
- Frequency scale: O(1 MHz) continuous sinusoidal waves (again, adjustable as needed to manage resolution/CFL).

### 3.2 Phase 1 – FDTD scripting (Python / MATLAB)

**Objective:** Prove the logic analytically/semianalytically using the 2D linear acoustic wave equation.

- Implement a simple finite‑difference time‑domain (FDTD) scheme for the linear acoustic wave equation in a rectangular domain containing the Y‑junction.  
- Drive two continuous sinusoidal sources at the inlets with controllable phase difference \(\phi\).  
- Demonstrate that varying \(\phi\) switches between constructive and destructive interference at the outlet.  
- Map specific phase settings to logical operations (e.g. AND = high amplitude, XOR = near‑zero amplitude) and quantify contrast ratios.

### 3.3 Phase 2 – OpenFOAM CFD baseline (rhoPimpleFoam)

**Objective:** Validate that the same logic is observable in a more realistic fluid simulation with proper boundaries and numerics.

- Solver: **rhoPimpleFoam** (compressible transient, suitable for acoustic waves at low Mach).  
- Recreate the Y‑junction geometry and inlet forcing within OpenFOAM.  
- Carefully choose mesh resolution and time step to resolve several points per wavelength and maintain a stable CFL.  
- **Critical component:** validate the **waveTransmissive** outlet boundary condition so that outgoing waves do not reflect and corrupt interference patterns.

### 3.4 Numerical acceptance criteria (Y‑junction)

Define simple metrics that must be met before scaling up:

- Reflection coefficient at outlet below a chosen threshold (e.g. < a few percent).  
- Numerical damping per wavelength small enough that interference patterns are stable over the domain length.  
- Phase error over a given propagation distance within acceptable bounds.  
- Logic contrast (ratio of "high" vs "low" output states) exceeding a minimum value.


## 4. Scaling to circuits – diffractive approach

Goal: Move from a single unit cell (Y‑junction) to **small analog circuits** implemented as diffractive wave propagation through structured media.

### 4.1 Circuit architecture principles

1. **Diffractive layers instead of plumbing**  
   - Treat extended regions of the domain as computational layers.  
   - Obstacle geometry (position, size, shape) implements effective weights by delaying and scattering waves.  
   - Multiple layers in sequence form a small diffractive network.

2. **Managing back‑reflection and directionality**  
   - Explore gentle background flow (advection) or non‑reflecting boundaries to preferentially support forward propagation of information.  
   - Aim to minimise unwanted echoes that would make cascading layers unstable or difficult to interpret.

3. **From hand‑designed to tuned geometries**  
   - Start with hand‑designed obstacle fields that qualitatively route energy.  
   - Then introduce a small number of tunable parameters (e.g. a few obstacle positions/radii) as a bridge to optimisation experiments.

> Note: Earlier ideas about topological insulator‑style edge states are acknowledged in the literature but kept **out of scope** for this master’s, to maintain tractability.


## 5. 3D roadmap and scope management

The 3D stage is treated as a **stretch goal** rather than a core deliverable.

### 5.1 Planned progression

1. **Step 1 – Solver baseline (2D Y‑junction)**  
   - Deliverable: validated Y‑junction case in both FDTD and OpenFOAM, with quantified logical behaviour and numerical error bounds.

2. **Step 2 – Diffractive slice (2D continuous medium)**  
   - Domain: 2D plane with random or structured array of solid cylinders/obstacles.  
   - Task: inject a continuous wave (or small set of inputs) and measure spatial distribution at outlet/probes.  
   - Deliverable: demonstration that spatial obstacle patterns can route and focus energy, implementing a small computational mapping.

3. **Step 3 – Target medium (3D porous matrix, stretch)**  
   - Extrude or generalise the validated 2D diffractive logic into 3D using rhoPimpleFoam.  
   - Focus on one or two simple 3D configurations that show qualitatively similar behavior to the 2D cases.  
   - If computational cost is prohibitive, document limitations and treat 3D results as preliminary.

### 5.2 Scope and fallback plan

- **Core master’s deliverables (must‑have):**  
  - 2D Y‑junction logic unit test (FDTD + OpenFOAM) with validation.  
  - At least one 2D diffractive slice acting as a small analog network, with basic parameter sensitivity analysis.  
  - A minimal optimisation study on a low‑dimensional parameter space.

- **Stretch goals (nice‑to‑have if time allows):**  
  - 3D porous simulation demonstrating a similar computational effect.  
  - More advanced optimisation or training schemes.

- **Fallback:**  
  - If 3D is infeasible, deepen the 2D work: multiple tasks, robustness to noise/perturbations, richer parameter sweeps, and comparison to simple digital baselines.


## 6. Minimal optimisation / "training" plan

Goal: Demonstrate that geometry or medium parameters can be **numerically tuned** to improve computational performance, without requiring full differentiable training inside OpenFOAM.

### 6.1 Design space

- Choose a small set of parameters: e.g.  
  - Positions and radii of a handful of cylinders in the diffractive slice.  
  - A few scalar porosity parameters for a simple porous region.  
- Keep dimensionality low (2–10 parameters) to make brute‑force or simple evolutionary search feasible.

### 6.2 Objective functions

Examples:

- Maximise time‑averaged energy at a target output probe while minimising energy at others.  
- Minimise error between the observed output pattern and a desired target pattern.  
- Simple classification task (e.g. two input patterns, two desired outputs) with a misclassification cost.

### 6.3 Optimisation strategy

- Use simple methods appropriate for a master’s thesis:  
  - Coarse grid search over the parameter space.  
  - Random search or basic evolutionary algorithm.  
- Optionally, accelerate evaluation using a cheaper FDTD surrogate, then validate best candidates in OpenFOAM.


## 7. Possible physical concept (optional)

Goal: Provide at least a **conceptual bridge** from simulation to a physical device.

### 7.1 Conceptual design

- Translate one successful simulated architecture (Y‑junction or small diffractive slice) into physical dimensions, materials, and operating frequencies.  
- Sketch or CAD a macro‑scale device (e.g. 3D‑printed channels in water or air) with actuator and sensor placement.

### 7.2 Low‑risk experimental idea (if allowed and feasible)

- Build a simple acoustic Y‑junction with a speaker at each inlet and microphone(s) at the outlet.  
- Vary relative phase between the inlets and show qualitative constructive/destructive interference consistent with simulations.  
- Treat this as a proof‑of‑concept demonstration rather than a high‑precision experiment.
