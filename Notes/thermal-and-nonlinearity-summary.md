## Scope

This note documents two interconnected research streams explored on 2026-05-31:

1. **Thermal effects** — How optothermal reconfiguration (laser heating) can dynamically alter wave propagation in a porous medium.
2. **Nonlinearity** — Whether porous media exhibit nonlinear acoustic behavior (they do, often strongly).

These two streams converge: **thermal gradients are themselves a source of nonlinearity**, and **bubble nucleation from heating creates extreme nonlinearity**.

## Part 1: Thermal Effects — Optothermal Reconfiguration

### User Requirements (from Q&A)
- Thermal effects should be a **trainable computational resource**, not just dissipation to model.
- Reconfiguration via **external lasers / optics** (non-contact, projected heating patterns).
- Explore three physical effects: **sound-speed change**, **density change**, and **bubble nucleation**.
- Start with **static programming** (heat, equilibrate, compute), stretch to **dynamic** (real-time reconfiguration).
- Full Biot poroelasticity required (fast P, slow P, shear waves).

### Physical Effects of Localised Heating

| Effect | Mechanism | Mathematical Form | Timescale | Reversibility |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|-----------|---------------|
| **Sound-speed change** | c(T) for most fluids decreases with T | $c(T) = c_0(1 - \alpha_T \Delta T)$ | ms (thermal diffusion) | Perfect |
| **Density change** | Thermal expansion → $\rho(T)$ | $\rho(T) = \rho_0(1 - \beta_T \Delta T)$ | ms | Perfect |
| **Viscosity change** | $\mu(T)$ drops sharply with T | Arrhenius-type | ms | Perfect |
| **Bubble nucleation** | Superheat → vapor bubbles in pores | Nucleation threshold $T_{sat}(P) + \Delta T_{super}$ | μs (nucleation) | Imperfect (bubbles dissolve) |
| **Thermal stress** | Expansion mismatch → solid prestress | $\sigma_{th} = E \alpha_{solid} \Delta T$ | ms | Perfect |
| **Convection** | Buoyancy-driven flow from $\nabla T$ | Rayleigh-Bénard in pores | 100 ms–s | Perfect |

### Key Design Question: How Localised Can Heating Be?

Thermal diffusion in water: $\alpha \approx 0.14 \times 10^{-6}$ m²/s.
Diffusion length in 10 ms: $L_d = \sqrt{\alpha t} \approx 37$ μm.

This sets the **spatial resolution** of your "thermal weights." If you want 100 μm features, you need heating pulses ~50 ms or less, or you need to accept some thermal blur.

### Thermal vs. Acoustic Timescales

| Process | Timescale | Implication |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Acoustic wave period @ 1 MHz | 1 μs | Much faster than thermal — waves see a "frozen" temperature field |
| Thermal diffusion across 1 mm | ~7 s | Slow enough for static programming |
| Bubble nucleation & growth | 1–100 μs | Comparable to acoustic period — potential for resonant coupling |
| Bubble dissolution | 10 ms–1 s | Limits dynamic reconfiguration rate |

**Conclusion:** Static thermal programming is clean — the acoustic field sees a fixed temperature landscape. Dynamic reconfiguration is possible but limited by bubble lifetime and thermal diffusion.

## Part 2: Nonlinearity in Porous Media

### The Short Answer

Porous media are **not inherently linear**. They are linear only under the small-amplitude approximation. At finite amplitudes — or with bubbles, thermal gradients, or prestress — porous media exhibit **rich nonlinear behavior**, often orders of magnitude stronger than homogeneous fluids or solids.

### Mechanism 1: Nonlinear Biot Poroelasticity

**Reference:** Tong et al. (2017), *J. Acoust. Soc. Am.* 142(2), 756–770  
**Title:** "Nonlinear wave propagation in porous materials based on the Biot theory"

Biot's 1956 theory is linear, but Tong et al. introduced **nonlinear elastic constants** into the Lagrangian formulation:

**Key findings:**
- Three new **coupled nonlinear parameters** (γ₁, γ₂, γ₃) describe solid–fluid coupling nonlinearity
- A **double-frequency nonlinear wave** is generated by coupling between linear fast and slow waves
- When only a shear wave is applied, three nonlinear longitudinal waves are generated
- The nonlinear displacement can exceed the linear displacement by >10 dB at certain distances
- The **nonlinear parameters of porous materials are orders of magnitude higher** than in pure water or solid

**Equation structure (1D longitudinal):**
$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c_L^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \gamma_1 \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \gamma_2 \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\frac{\partial w}{\partial x} + \gamma_3 \left(\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\right)^2$$

where $u$ = solid displacement, $w$ = fluid displacement. The cross terms γ₂, γ₃ are unique to porous media.

### Mechanism 2: Forchheimer Inertial Nonlinearity

**References:**
- Moufid et al. (2022), ENOC 2021
- Umnova et al. (2003), *JASA* 114(3), 1346–1356
- Turno & Umnova (2013), *JASA* 134(6), 4763–4774

At low amplitudes, flow resistance follows Darcy's law (linear in velocity). At high amplitudes, the **Forchheimer correction** dominates:

$$\sigma = \sigma_0(1 + \xi \phi |u|)$$

This adds a **quadratic velocity term** to the momentum equation:

$$\rho_0 \alpha_\infty \partial_t u + \nabla p + Mu + N(g \star \partial_t u) = -F_\xi |u|u$$

**Experimental evidence:** Rigid porous absorbers at >140 dB show amplitude-dependent absorption. NASA (1985) documented this for jet engine inlet ducts.

### Mechanism 3: Bubbles in Porous Media — Extreme Nonlinearity

**References:**
- Donskoy & Sutin (1984), *Sov. Phys. Acoust.* 30, 358
- Boyle & Chotiros (1998), *JASA* 103(5), 1328
- Ostrovsky, ASA 139th Meeting lecture
- Pham et al. (2020), *J. Fluid Mech.*

Even a **tiny bubble volume fraction** increases the nonlinearity parameter B/A by **several orders of magnitude**:

| Medium | B/A (nonlinearity parameter) |
|--------|------------------------------|
| Pure water | ~5 |
| Water with 0.1% bubbles | ~50–500 |
| Sandy sediment (saturated) | ~80 @ 38 kHz |
| Granular porous media | Up to 10⁴ |

**Physical mechanisms:**
- **Rayleigh-Plesset bubble oscillation:** nonlinear radial dynamics
- **Bubble resonance:** strong frequency-dependent scattering near Minnaert frequency
- **Bubble-bubble interaction:** collective behavior changes macroscopic nonlinear properties
- **Phase conjugation:** demonstrated in marine sediments via nonlinear bubble scattering

**Pham et al. (2020)** showed that a thin bubble screen in water behaves as a **nonlinear metasurface** with jump conditions across the screen, governed by a Rayleigh-Plesset-type equation.

### Mechanism 4: Acoustoelasticity — Prestress-Dependent Wave Speeds

**Reference:** Norris (1996), *JASA* 100(3), 1368–1374

In a prestressed porous medium, wave speeds change with applied stress/strain. Three sources:

1. **Physical nonlinearity** — nonlinear constitutive law $\sigma(\varepsilon)$
2. **Geometric nonlinearity** — finite deformation gradient
3. **Universal equation nonlinearity** — nonlinear conservation of mass/momentum

**Implication for your project:** If thermal expansion creates mechanical stress in the scaffold, the wave speeds change nonlinearly with that stress. This is a **second-order thermal nonlinearity**.

### Mechanism 5: Thermoviscous Nonlinearity in Narrow Pores

**Reference:** Sahithi & Gupta (2024), arXiv:2402.09889

In narrow pores with wavy walls, nonlinear wave steepening competes with thermoviscous dissipation. The wall heat-flux scales as:
- Flat walls: $q_w \propto \epsilon$ (linear in wave strength)
- Wavy walls: $q_w \propto \epsilon^{3/2 - n(k)}$ where $n(k)$ increases with wall waviness

Increasing wall waviness **reduces** the dependence of heat flux on nonlinear strength — an unexpected geometric effect.

## Part 3: Convergence — Thermal + Nonlinear = Trainable Computation

### How These Two Streams Interact

```
[Laser heating pattern T(x)]
         │
         ├──► c(T), ρ(T), μ(T)  ──► Linear refractive index change
         │
         ├──► Thermal stress σ_th(T) ──► Acoustoelastic wave-speed shift (nonlinear)
         │
         ├──► Superheat > T_sat ──► Bubble nucleation (strongly nonlinear)
         │
         └──► ∇T ──► Natural convection ──► Flow-modified wave paths
```

### Trainable Nonlinearity: The Key Insight

In a standard neural network, the **activation function** is fixed (ReLU, tanh, sigmoid). In your porous wave computer:

| NN Component | Porous Medium Analog | Control Mechanism |
|--------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Weights | Pore geometry + c(T) distribution | Laser heating pattern |
| Linear transform | Wave propagation + interference | Fixed scaffold + thermal index |
| **Activation function** | **Forchheimer nonlinearity + bubble resonance + c(T) nonlinearity** | **Laser intensity + temperature** |
| Bias | Background pressure / static temperature | Chamber conditions |

**The nonlinearity is itself trainable.** By adjusting the local temperature field, you control:
- Whether the Forchheimer term is active (high amplitude, high T → lower viscosity → different Reynolds number)
- Whether bubbles exist (threshold nonlinearity)
- The strength of thermal lensing (gradient-index effect)

### Static vs. Dynamic Reconfiguration of Nonlinearity

| Mode | What Changes | Timescale | Physics |
|------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| **Static** | Temperature field T(x) is fixed during computation | ms to set, μs to compute | Linear + weakly nonlinear waves in fixed T(x) |
| **Dynamic** | T(x,t) evolves during wave propagation | μs–ms | Fully nonlinear, time-varying medium; waves interact with moving thermal front |

## Part 4: Implications for Architecture Choice

### Architecture A (Fixed Scaffold + Thermal Fluid)
- **Linearity:** Weakly nonlinear at moderate amplitudes (Forchheimer, Tong's coupled terms)
- **Thermal nonlinearity:** c(T) creates mild nonlinearity; thermal stress adds acoustoelastic shift
- **Controllable nonlinearity:** Yes — by tuning T(x) and source amplitude
- **Suitable for:** Static programming with weakly nonlinear activation

### Architecture B (Bubble Cloud = Porosity)
- **Linearity:** Strongly nonlinear (bubble resonance dominates)
- **Thermal nonlinearity:** Bubble nucleation is a threshold process — inherently nonlinear
- **Controllable nonlinearity:** Yes — bubble density φ(x) directly controls B/A
- **Suitable for:** Strongly nonlinear computation, but harder to control

### Architecture C (Hybrid)
- **Linearity:** All of the above combined
- **Thermal nonlinearity:** Maximum richness — c(T) + bubble + stress + Forchheimer
- **Controllable nonlinearity:** Most flexible, but most complex
- **Suitable for:** Ambitious stretch goal
